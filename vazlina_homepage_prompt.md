# Homepage Design Prompt — Vazlina (Mexico COD)

**Reference Website:** https://namabeauty.shop/
**Goal:** Replicate the exact structure, trust architecture, badge placement, and branded feel of NAMA Beauty, but adapted for Vazlina (gadget brand) in Mexico.

---

## Part 1: Global Design Language (Copy NAMA's Branded Feel)

### Typography
- Headlines: Bold, tight letter-spacing, authoritative. Not playful.
- Body: Clean sans-serif, generous line-height for readability.
- Numbers (prices, reviews, stats): Larger than surrounding text, bold weight.

### Color Palette (Branded, Not Generic)
- **Primary (Headlines/CTAs):** Deep charcoal or off-black. Avoid pure #000000.
- **Secondary (Accents/Badges):** A single signature color (e.g., warm sage green or soft terracotta). Use sparingly — only for CTAs and one accent element.
- **Background:** Warm off-white (not stark white). Creates a premium, paper-like feel.
- **Trust bar background:** Slightly darker shade of the off-white, or very light version of the accent color.
- **Badges:** Monochrome or single-color line icons. NEVER use colorful generic trust badge packs.

### Icon Style
- Minimal line icons or simple geometric shapes.
- One color (same as text or accent color).
- NO gradients, NO 3D effects, NO stock-looking shield badges.
- Examples: A simple checkmark inside a circle, a small truck outline, a shield outline.

## Part 3: What Makes This Branded (Key Instructions)

1. **No generic Shopify badges.** The trust elements in NAMA are CUSTOM text with minimal icons. Do not use colorful 3D shield icons. Do not use "Secure Checkout" badges with credit card logos. Mexico COD customers don't care about Stripe logos — they care about "Pago Contra Entrega" and "Soporte Local."

2. **One accent color only.** NAMA uses a clinical, cohesive palette. Do not use 5 different colors for different sections. Pick ONE accent (warm sage green or muted terracotta) and use it ONLY for CTAs, the step numbers, and quotation marks.

3. **Headlines speak to the customer.** NAMA's headlines are emotional and specific: "beauty that starts from within," "a pharmacy, not a beauty store." Vazlina headlines should be equally specific: "gadgets that solve real problems," "a brand, not a marketplace."

4. **Testimonials include specific details.** NAMA testimonials cite age, city, and verified status. Vazlina testimonials should do the same. Generic testimonials feel fake. Specific details feel real.

5. **FAQ addresses the real objections.** NAMA's FAQ answers questions about Halal, SFDA, COD availability, and delivery time. Vazlina's FAQ should answer the #1 Mexico COD objection: "Is this a scam?" plus delivery, returns, and why buy here vs. Amazon.

6. **The 3-step process is COD-native.** NAMA's steps assume COD: "confirm without payment," "receive and pay." Vazlina's steps must do the same. Do not mention credit cards anywhere in the steps.

7. **Typography is the brand.** NAMA feels premium because of typography choices: bold headlines, generous spacing, clean hierarchy. Do not cram elements together. White space IS the brand.

## Part 4: Technical Notes for Developer

- **Sticky footer trust bar:** Must be visible on scroll, especially on mobile.
- **Accordion FAQ:** Only one question open at a time. Smooth animation.
- **Product cards:** On hover, image slightly zooms (subtle). No other animation needed.
- **CTA buttons:** On hover, background darkens slightly. No fancy effects.
- **Mobile:** All grids become single-column stacked. Trust bar becomes 2x2 grid.
- **No popups on homepage.** No spin-to-win. No newsletter signup overlays. These destroy trust for COD brands.
- **All text in Mexican Spanish.** Not Spain Spanish. "Pedido" not "encargo." "Celular" not "movil." "Corre" not "funciona."
